# What is a Magnetic Field?
- The magnetic field, B, is a field created by **moving charges** and 
  **current-carrying conductors** that exerts forces on other moving charges 
  and current-carrying conductors.
- The magnetic field is NOT a variation of the electric field. It is a genuinely 
  distinct physical entity with its own sources, its own force law, and its own 
  geometry. Together, electric and magnetic fields form the unified theory of 
  electromagnetism.
- The magnetic field is a **vector quantity** — it has both magnitude and 
  direction at every point in space, denoted **B**.
- The SI unit of magnetic field is the **Tesla:**
$$1\,\text{T} = 1\,\frac{\text{N}}{\text{A}\cdot\text{m}}$$

## Representing Magnetic Fields on Paper
- Since magnetic fields exist in 3D space, we need conventions for representing 
  their direction on a 2D page:
  - **Out of the page:** Represented by dots (·) — like the tip of an arrow 
    coming toward you
  - **Into the page:** Represented by crosses (×) — like the tail feathers of 
    an arrow going away from you


# Magnetic Field Lines
  - Field lines point in the direction of **B** at every point
  - The density of field lines indicates field strength — closer lines = 
    stronger field
  - **Magnetic field lines ALWAYS form closed loops** — they never start or end 
    on a source the way electric field lines start and end on charges
- This last point is profound. It means:
  > **There are no magnetic monopoles** — no isolated north or south poles 
  > analogous to positive and negative charges.
- Every magnet has both a north and south pole. Cut a magnet in half and you 
  get two magnets, each with both poles. Field lines always close on themselves.
- This is expressed mathematically as **Gauss's Law for Magnetism** — 
  Maxwell's second equation:
$$\oint \vec{B} \cdot d\vec{A} = 0$$
- The total magnetic flux through any closed surface is always zero. For every 
  field line that exits a closed surface, one must re-enter — because field 
  lines are closed loops with no sources or sinks.

# The Magnetic Force on a Moving Charge

## General Formula
$$\boxed{\vec{F}_B = q\vec{v} \times \vec{B}}$$
- Where:
  - $q$ — charge of the particle **with sign**
  - $\vec{v}$ — velocity of the particle
  - $\vec{B}$ — magnetic field at the particle's location
  - $\times$ — the **cross product** (not multiplication)
- The magnitude of this force:
$$F_B = |q|vB\sin\theta$$
- Where θ is the angle between **v** and **B**.
- This means that Magnetic Force strength is max when velocity and magnetic field are perpendicular. 


## Critical Properties of the Magnetic Force

### Property 1 — The Magnetic Force Does No Work
- The magnetic force is always **perpendicular to the velocity.** Since:
$$W = \vec{F} \cdot \vec{d} = Fd\cos\theta$$
- And the angle between $F_B$ and v is always 90°:
$$W_{magnetic} = 0 \quad \text{always}$$
- The magnetic force can change the **direction** of a particle's motion but 
  **never its speed or kinetic energy.** It redirects — it does not accelerate 
  or decelerate.
- IMPORTANT: If an AP exam question asks whether a magnetic field does work on 
  a moving charge, the answer is **always no.**

### Property 2 — Stationary Charges Feel No Magnetic Force
- From $F_B = |q|vB\sin\theta$ — if $v = 0$, then $F_B = 0$.
- A charge **must be moving** to experience a magnetic force. This is why 
  static electric charges don't interact with magnets.


# Circular Motion in a Magnetic Field

## The Setup
- When a charged particle moves perpendicular to a uniform magnetic field, the magnetic force is always perpendicular to the velocity,  pointing toward the center of a circular path. This is exactly the condition for uniform circular motion, a concept tested in Mechanics.
- The magnetic force provides the centripetal force:
$$|q|vB = \frac{mv^2}{r}$$
- When solving for radius, we are left with what we call the Cyclotron Radius…
$$\boxed{r = \frac{mv}{|q|B}}$$
- Notice:
  - Larger mass or speed → larger circle
  - Stronger field or larger charge → smaller circle

- When we loop in equations for period and frequency…
$$T = \frac{2\pi r}{v} = \frac{2\pi}{v} \cdot \frac{mv}{|q|B}$$
$$\boxed{T = \frac{2\pi m}{|q|B}}$$
$$\boxed{f = \frac{|q|B}{2\pi m}}$$
- The period T and frequency f are independent of speed and radius. A faster particle travels a larger circle but completes one revolution in exactly the same time as a slower particle in a smaller circle.

# Helical Motion
- When a particle that has a velocity component parallel and perpendicular to the Magnetic Field, it will begin to spiral, and move in a helix. The velocity component parallel to the magnetic field has no effect on the radius of the helix, only the perpendicular component, given by…
$$r = \frac{mv_\perp}{|q|B}$$

## Condition for Straight-Line Travel
- For a particle to pass through undeflected, the two forces must exactly cancel:
$$F_E = F_B$$
$$qE = qvB$$
$$\boxed{v = \frac{E}{B}}$$
- Only particles with exactly this speed travel straight through. Faster 
  particles are deflected one way, slower particles the other.
- IMPORTANT: The selected speed depends **only on E and B** — not on charge or 
  mass. This makes the velocity selector a universal speed filter for any 
  charged particle.
