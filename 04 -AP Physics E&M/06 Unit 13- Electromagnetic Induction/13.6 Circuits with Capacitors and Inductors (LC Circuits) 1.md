# Concepts
_________________
An LC circuit with no resistance oscillates indefinitely – energy sloshes back and forth between the electric field of the capacitor and the magnetic field of the inductor. This is the electromagnetic analog of frictionless mass-spring system performing simple harmonic motion. 

Remove a resistor from an LR circuit and replace it with a capacitor – you now have an LC circuit. With no resistance to dissipate energy, the system oscillates at a fixed frequency forever (must be ideal). This is electromagnetic simple harmonic motion.
- In Circuits containing only a charged capacitor and an inductor (LC Circuit) the time dependence of the charge stored in the capacitor can be modeled as simple harmonic motion.

#### LC Circuits  
- Circuits with only an inductor (L) and Capacitor (C) are known as LC Circuits
- 

| Capacitors                                  | Inductors                                    |
| ------------------------------------------- | -------------------------------------------- |
| Schematic Symbol: C                         | Schematic Symbol: L                          |
| Unit: Farad (F)                             | Unit: Henry (H)                              |
| Equation: $$C=\frac{\kappa \epsilon A}{d}$$ | Equation: $$L=\frac{\mu_{core}N^2A}{l}$$     |
| Stores energy in its electric field         | Stores energy in its magnetic field          |

| Initial charging acts like “wire”           | Initial Current acts like “opposing battery” |
| Steady charge acts like “opposing battery”  | Steady current acts like “wire”              |
#### Solving for Maximum Current
*In circuits containing only a charged capacitor and an inductor, the maximum current through the inductor can be determined using conservation of energy within the circuit.* $$U_{Cmax}=U_{C}+U_{L}=U_{Lmax}$$
$$\frac{1}{2}C \Delta V_{max}^2 = \frac{1}{2}LI_{max}^2$$

### Kirchhoff’s Derivation of the LC Differential Equation
#### Kirchhoff’s Voltage Law around the loop (no Resistance):
$$V_{C}+V_{L}=0$$ $$\frac{q}{C}=L \frac{dI}{dt}=0$$
$$\begin{array}
 & Since \space I=\frac{dq}{dt}, \space we \space know \space \frac{dI}{dt}=\frac{d^2q}{dt^2}
\end{array}$$
$$L \frac{d^2q}{dt^2}+\frac{q}{C}=0$$
$$\frac{d^2q}{dt^2}=-\frac{1}{LC}q$$
#### LC Circuit Behavior
- E-Field in capacitor generates a current in the circuit
- Flowing current creates a B-field in the inductor
- Collapsing B-field causes current to flow
- Current charges capacitor generating an E-field
