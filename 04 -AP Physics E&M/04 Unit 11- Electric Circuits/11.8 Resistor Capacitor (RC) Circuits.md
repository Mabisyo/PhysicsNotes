# What is an RC Circuit?
- An RC is a circuit that includes both a resistor and capacitor in the same circuit, along with a battery and/or switch. Since capacitors take time to charge, RC circuits create time varying currents and voltages. The capacitor charges and discharges over a time tau.
- Since we now have time as a factor, we need to discuss the existence of 2 main times…
	- t=0, such as when a switch just closes, or a battery is just connected. This means capacitors will begin charging or discharging.
	- t=infinity, also known as steady state. This means capacitors are either fully charged, or fully discharged.
	- t=x, we can plug our time values into our derived equations, and solve for the value of capacitance or resistance.


# Series Circuit
![[rcseriescircuitexampleteach.png]]
- In the following situation, a resistor and capacitor are in series, meaning that we can derive an equation for the charging and unchanging of a capacitor.

1. If we assume Kirchhoff’s Loop rule to be clockwise, starting with the battery with a voltage of epsilon, then…
$$V_{\epsilon}-V_R-V_C=0$$
$$\epsilon-IR-\frac{Q}{C}=0$$
$$\epsilon-R\frac{dQ}{dt}-\frac{Q}{C}=0$$
2. By rearraigning our equation to separate variables…
$$\epsilon-\frac{Q}{C}=R\frac{dQ}{dt}$$
$$\frac{C\epsilon-Q}{C}=R\frac{dQ}{dt}$$
$$\frac{dQ}{C\epsilon-Q}=\frac{dt}{RC}$$
3. By setting up integrals and integrating (Using a U-sub on the left) with bounds from 0 to Q and 0 to t, we get…
$$\int_0^Q{\frac{dQ}{C\epsilon-Q}}=\int^t_0{\frac{dt}{RC}}$$
$$\left[-\ln{|C\epsilon-Q|} \right]^Q_0=\left[\frac{t}{RC}\right]^R_0$$
$$-\ln |C\epsilon-Q|+\ln|C\epsilon|=\frac{t}{RC}$$
4. Now, we can isolate Q to find the charge of a capacitor in terms of t, that depends on the resistance…
$$\ln \left|\frac{C\epsilon}{C\epsilon-Q}\right|=\frac{t}{RC}$$
$$\frac{C\epsilon}{C\epsilon-Q}=e^{\frac{t}{RC}}$$
- In this step, reciprocating both sides leads to easier algebra, and will make the exponent negative
$$\frac{C\epsilon-Q}{C\epsilon}=e^{-\frac{t}{RC}}$$
$$C\epsilon-Q=C\epsilon e^{-\frac{t}{RC}}$$
$$Q_C(t)=C\epsilon\left( 1-e^{-\frac{t}{RC}}\right)$$
5. From this equation, we can derive with respect to time to get the equation for current…
$$I(t)=\frac{\epsilon}{R}e^{-t/RC}$$
- With Ohms law, we can find the Voltage for both the capacitor and the resistor, being…
$$V_C(t)=\epsilon (1-e^{-t/RC})$$
$$V_C(t)=\epsilon (e^{-t/RC})$$
Following this same process, we can find the equations for discharging, or when the battery is disconnected…
$$Q(t)=Q_oe^{-t/RC}$$
$$I(t)=\frac{Q_o}{RC}e^{-t/RC}=I_oe^{-t/RC}$$
$$V_C=\epsilon e^{-t/RC}$$


# Parallel
- Since current takes the path of least resistance, the current is going to travel the most through the wire with the least resistance. This means that the current through the resistor and capacitor are variable, depending on how much charge is in the capacitor. 

- At t=0, then
	- All the current goes through the capacitor, so I=V/R for the capacitor, but I=0 for the resistor.

- At t= infinity, then
	- All the current goes through the resistor, since the capacitor has infinite resistance, so I=0 for the capacitor, and I=V/R for the resistor. 

- Because both the capacitor and the resistor are in parallel, their voltages are equal for all values of t.


# Time Constant
$$\tau=RC$$
- Where Tau is the time constant, R is the resistance, and C is the capacitance.
- We use Tau as a proportion to measure what percent the capacitor has charged/discharged based on percentages.

|   t=…   | Charging, % of Q | Discharging % remaining |
|:-------:| ---------------- | ----------------------- |
| 1$\tau$ | 63.2%            | 36.8%                   |
| 2$\tau$ | 86.5%            | 13.5%                   |
| 3$\tau$ | 95.0%            | 5.0%                    |
| 5$\tau$ | 99.3%            | 0.7%                    |
- For mathematical reasons, 5tau is considered to be fully charged or discharged.
- We don’t need to precisely memorize these values, just understand that…
	- About 60% charged at 1tau, and 100% charged at 5tau
	- The greater the resistance and capacitance, the longer it takes to charge
	- The more charge accumulated in the capacitor, the longer it takes to charge, hence why the percentage values are concaved.



# Energy Considerations
- The total energy supplied by the battery is…
$$U_{battery}=C\epsilon^2$$

- The energy stored in a fully charged capacitor, as stated before, is…
$$U_C=\frac{1}{2}C\epsilon^2$$
- The energy dissipated by the resistor is…
$$U_R=\frac{1}{2}C\epsilon^2$$
- No matter how large or small the resistance, exactly half the battery's energy goes to the capacitor and half is lost to heat in the resistor. The resistance only controls how fast/slow this happens. A smaller R lets the heat loss happen faster, a larger R makes this process happen slower.
